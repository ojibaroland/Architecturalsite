How We Work

A process with no surprises.

  1. i.

    Listen

    Every engagement begins with a conversation led by Andrew — about the site, the brief, the budget, and what the building needs to do for the next fifty years.

  2. ii.

    Assess

    Feasibility, zoning, soils, and structural realities before any design is drawn. We tell you early if something can't or shouldn't be built.

  3. iii.

    Design & Engineer

    Architecture and engineering advance together, not in sequence — so the documents that leave the studio are buildable, priceable, and permit-ready.

  4. iv.

    Build

    Permitting, bidding, and construction administration — with the principal on site defending both the design and the structure until the keys are handed over.

  5. v.

    Stand Behind It

    A post-occupancy review after the building has lived through a season. Buildings teach; we make sure the lessons come home.

The Halland Advantage

Why one firm for design and engineering?

When the architect and the construction engineer are the same person, the usual failure points disappear: nothing is designed that can't be built, nothing is engineered that betrays the design, and no change order arrives because two firms weren't talking. The result is a straighter path from idea to occupancy — and a building whose beauty and bones were never in competition.
